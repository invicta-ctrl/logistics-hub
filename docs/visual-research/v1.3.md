# V1.3 visual research and evidence: USC Staff Directory

Spec: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/v1.3-staff-directory.md` (source amended by `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/2026-10-03-v1.3-google-drive-source-amendment.md`). Screenshots are in `docs/visual-research/v1.3/` (`before/` is `main` at V1.2, `after/` is this branch). Every person in them is fictional, and every ID card is an obviously fake "SAMPLE STUDENT COUNCIL" card drawn by the evidence script on a canvas at run time: a plain silhouette instead of a face, invented numbers, "SAMPLE" across it. No real scan, no image of a real ID and no personal data from the private source appears in this folder or anywhere in Git.

## How the evidence was made

- The real application on a throwaway local Worker, D1 and R2 (`wrangler dev --local`) with every migration applied (`0001`–`0021`), the two fictional demo accounts (Alex Reyes as Owner, Sam Cruz as Staff) and fictional loans and phone takes written the way the app writes them.
- `npm run evidence -- --out docs/visual-research/v1.3 --base origin/main --pages admin,account,staff-directory`. `admin` and `account` render on both refs (before and after compare). `staff-directory` runs only where the directory exists: it adds 24 fictional people (a Department of Logistics of 16, officers in every office, partial profiles with no position, one inactive person), draws the sample archive into a throwaway folder laid out as the Drive archive is (`[DEM] Official ID`, `[DoL] Official ID`, `[DHR] Official ID`, `OFFICERS`, one `desktop.ini`) with one of each problem the preflight reports, imports it **through the owner's real import screen**, and captures desktop (1440×900), tablet (768×1024, 2×) and phone (390×844, 2×). Reduced motion is on for stable frames, except for the 3D card sequence (tile lean, flight, tilt, turn), which runs with motion and freezes the flight at fixed times by pausing every running animation; the turn and zoom are exercised by keys.
- Every image was opened and looked at, not only asserted on. The first pass found real defects, listed below, which were fixed and captured again. Files are WebP (quality 72); unchanged menus and the tablet copies of the denser screens were dropped (2.4 MB).

## Patterns adopted, and why they fit Logistics Hub

- **Departments as panels of compact rows, in two columns (team directories).** The council has ten offices and departments of very different sizes (one President, a Department of Logistics of 16). The first pass used a card grid per department; with many one-person offices that left one card per row and a long scroll (defect 1). Panels of rows in CSS columns keep every office visible on one desktop screen, and the large department reads as a list. A single department (from the department index) spreads its rows over a grid.
- **Department tints, used only for initials and dots.** One tint and ink per code (ink on tint at least 7:1), so a department is recognisable in the list, the profile header and the import list without adding a new accent to the oxblood interface.
- **Only what is present gets a tag.** Officer, USC ID on file, the linked sign-in and Inactive. "No USC ID" on nearly every row was noise (defect 2); what is missing has its own filter chip with a count instead.
- **Profile as a summary list beside its side panel (GOV.UK).** Details on the left, the sign-in link (or recent ID openings, or the directory record) on the right on wide screens, stacked on phones.
- **Tabs with manual activation (APG).** Profile | USC ID | Usage | Loans | Activity load data, so arrows move and Enter opens; the open section is in the URL, so Back, reload and sharing a link keep it.
- **The card is one object you turn over (document tools).** The viewer shows one card in the middle of a dark stage, Front/Back as a two-state switch, and a 3D turn between the sides (a spring since Earl's 3D request, settled in about half a second; instant under reduced motion). Zoom 1–4× by wheel, pinch, double-click, + / − / Fit, pan by dragging or arrow keys; F and B turn it; Escape and Back close it. The first pass let the page show through the viewer (defect 4); the stage is now opaque, which also keeps the page behind out of view while an ID is open.
- **Who opened it, next to it (EHR access logs).** The USC ID section lists recent openings; the Activity section keeps the directory record (added, edited, linked, imported, opened). Every opening is also in Activity under "Staff Directory", once per viewer and card every ten minutes.
- **Import as a check before an upload.** The owner chooses the downloaded folder; the check runs on the device (names and hashes only) and shows what is ready, what needs a person and why, what is already in the directory and what was skipped, before anything is sent.

## What the screenshots showed, and what changed

1. **One card per row for one-person offices** (list, desktop). Replaced by department panels of compact rows in two columns.
2. **"No USC ID" on almost every card.** Removed; the filter chip carries the count.
3. **Phone list wider than the screen.** The single-column layout used `1fr`, which grew to the width of the scrolling department chips; now `minmax(0, 1fr)`. The accessibility checks now assert no sideways scroll at 320–1366 px and at 200% text.
4. **The page showed through the ID viewer**, and on phones the close button wrapped onto the zoom row. The viewer is opaque; on phones the title and close share the first row, Front/Back and zoom the second.
5. **The chosen department scrolled out of sight** in the phone/tablet chip row. It is scrolled into view.
6. **Usage "Area" label cut off** ("Inventory and |"). Now "All areas".
7. **Activity summaries as grey boxes** were heavy. Now the sentence is the line, with its type and time beneath.
8. **Profile sections a single narrow column on wide screens.** Now details and the side panel sit side by side from 1100 px.

## The 3D card (Earl, 2026-10-03: "3D animated whenever pressed and viewed, like Pokémon cards")

Earl asked for the ID card to move like a holographic trading card. It now does, in `src/card-motion.ts`:
- **Pressed:** a tile leans toward the mouse under a glare and a light foil, and presses in under a finger. Opening it, the card leaves its slot, flies to the middle turning once around its vertical axis and lifting toward you, then lands with a small overshoot while the page darkens. A single sweep of light then passes over it.
- **Viewed:** the card leans toward the pointer or a dragging finger (up to about 14°), with a glare where the light falls and a holographic foil sliding the other way. It presses in while held, turns over on a spring for Front and Back, and on closing flies back into the tile of the side it shows.

Sources read for this part (2026-10-03):

| Source | What I took from it |
|---|---|
| [simeydotme/pokemon-cards-css](https://github.com/simeydotme/pokemon-cards-css) (Simon Goellner; its `Card.svelte` and `cards/base.css` read as raw source) | The springs: following at stiffness 0.066 / damping 0.25, the looser return at 0.01 / 0.06. The tilt: the pointer's offset from the centre in percent divided by 3.5, so up to ±14.3°. The glare: a radial gradient at the pointer, blended with `overlay`. The foil: a gradient blended with `color-dodge`, its position moving across 37–63% / 33–67% as the pointer crosses the card. The pop-out: a 360° turn the first time a card comes forward. It has no reduced-motion handling. |
| [OpenReplay: creating holographic effects with CSS](https://blog.openreplay.com/creating-holographic-effects-css/) (search summary only) | `color-dodge` gives the metallic flash and `overlay` adds depth without washing out. Blend modes and filters cost GPU time on phones, so use few layers and no `filter`. Always honour `prefers-reduced-motion`. |
| [hover-tilt credit cards](https://hover-tilt.simey.me/bespoke/credit-cards/) | Not read: the session's network policy blocked the host. |

What changed for an ID card, and why:
- **Reading comes first.** The card rests flat and unlit, so the scan looks as scanned; the light only appears while someone moves over it. Zoomed in, it lies still: zoom is for reading fine print, and tilt would fight the pan. Under reduced motion nothing flies, leans, sweeps or spins: it opens in place and turns over at once, and the hint no longer mentions tilting.
- **The glare is much weaker than on a trading card** (about a third of pokemon-cards-css's white). On a trading card the glare can hide the artwork for a moment; on an ID it fell on the photo, which is what people point at.
- **The flight connects the card to its place**, by FLIP: it leaves the tile it was pressed on and returns to the tile of the side it shows, with the page behind darkening and lightening along the way. That makes clear which card is open and where it went.
- **Everything moves through CSS custom properties and Web Animations.** The Content-Security-Policy (`style-src 'self'`) blocks inline style attributes. The foil and glare are card-sized gradients, with no `filter` and no oversized layers. The springs run only while something moves, and the frame loop stops at rest.

What the rendered frames showed, and what changed (frames frozen at exact times by pausing every running animation):

9. **The flight finished in a blur.** It was 87% done by 280 ms, so the turn could not be seen. The launch spring is softer now: 95% of the way at 500 ms, settled by 1.3 s. The viewer takes input and the light sweeps at 95%, not at the end of the spring's tail.
10. **The glare washed out the photo** when the pointer sat on it. It was lowered twice, and the foil to 32%.
11. **A turning card was clipped** at the edge of its stage (its near edge grows toward you). The stage now lets it overflow unless zoomed.
12. **The viewer's title overlapped the page header** while the bar faded out on close. It now goes in 90 ms, and on opening it comes in after the page has darkened.
13. **The page showed through the viewer** during and after the flight: the backdrop is clear while the card flies, and the viewer's own 97% was not enough. The flying viewer darkens to 99% (the page differs from the stage by 2 of 255 levels in the phone frame).
14. **The card's shadow popped off** when it landed back in the tile. The shadow now grows and shrinks with the flight.
15. **Found while testing: a portrait ID would show in a landscape tile in production.** The tile took its shape from an inline style attribute, which the live Content-Security-Policy drops. It is now set through the CSSOM. The Worker end-to-end test imports a portrait sample under the real policy, checks the tile's shape and that the console has no policy violation, and fails with the old attribute.

## The card wall (Earl, 2026-10-03: the USC ID is what you press to see everything)

Earl's point: the card had become something you reach through a profile and a tab, so it had lost its purpose. Now the directory *is* the cards. Each person is an upright ID-1 card on a wall grouped by department. Pressing one lifts it out in 3D and turns it over to their details, and from there it turns on to both sides of their official ID.

Sources (accessed 2026-10-03):
- [Apple Wallet: creating a generic pass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/creating-a-generic-pass.md). A pass carries a few header fields, one primary field, secondary and auxiliary fields, and back fields for the details that do not belong on the front. Text sits in fields, never baked into images.
- [shadcn/ui "Filterable department team grid"](https://www.shadcnblocks.com/block/team45), [UW–Madison people listing](https://uwtheme.brand.wisc.edu/blocks/uw-people-listing/), [MangoApps people directory views](https://guides.mangoapps.com/user-guide/people-directory-and-profiles/classic-vs.-modern-view). People directories use a tile view for recognising faces and roles, a department colour for grouping at a glance, and search and filters above the grid.
- pokemon-cards-css (already cited above) for the tilt, the glare, and foil reserved for the rare cards.

Adopted, and why it fits:
- **Front = header and primary fields; back = the details.** On the front: department colour band and code, initials where a photo goes, then name, position and department, plus at most three marks (Officer foil, ID on file, sign-in). On the back: student number, sign-in, USC ID, status, what they have **on loan now** (the logistics question), and one tap to Profile, Usage, Loans or Activity. The fields are real text, so search, screen readers and 200% text work.
- **Drawn from the record, never from a scan.** A wall of 96 people loading 96 private scans would be a bulk exposure, and every load would be an opening to record. The wall is built from directory fields only. Opening a card fetches no scan. A scan loads only when someone turns the card to a side (or opens the USC ID section), and that opening is recorded as before. This keeps the spec's rule that ID imagery is more sensitive than the directory itself.
- **One card, three faces.** Details, Front and Back sit in one switch (D, F, B, or the arrow keys). Each turn puts the next view on the face underneath, so a physical two-sided card shows three things without ever showing the wrong face. The face that is not up is `inert` and hidden from assistive technology.
- **The flight tells the story.** The card leaves its tile showing the front and lands one and a half turns later on the details: the reveal. Closing flies it back, turning to the face the tile shows, so it lands exactly as it left. Under reduced motion it opens in place.
- **Foil means rank.** Only officers' cards carry the holographic foil, faint at rest and catching colour under the mouse, like a rare card. The ordinary glare uses soft-light so it never washes out the name. A first version used overlay and color-dodge on every card and greyed the hovered card's text (found in the rendered hover frame).
- **Practical defaults.** A plain press opens the card; a press with Ctrl or ⌘ still follows the card's link to the full profile in a new tab. A section link on the card closes it and opens that section, and Back returns to the wall. On a profile, the card beside the name opens straight on the ID.

What the rendered frames showed, and what changed:

16. **The first front was left-aligned with a small photo**, and the lower half of every card stood empty. It is now centred, with a larger photo and name, which fills the card the way an ID badge does.
17. **The profile's card was too small to hold the front's text**, which overflowed. Beside the name it keeps only the band, the initials and the name.
18. **Turning from the upright details to a landscape scan** made the card jump to its new shape. The card now changes shape over the turn (380 ms; not under reduced motion).
19. **The viewer's backdrop**, re-measured from the frame because the preview looked translucent: no pixel of the page area is above 15 of 255 behind the 99% stage.

Screenshots (`after/`, fictional people and sample card): `directory-list-*`, `directory-department-*` and `directory-search-*` (the wall), `directory-profile-*` (the card beside the name), `directory-card-details-*` (a card pressed, on the details), and `directory-card-front-*` (turned to the front of the ID).

## Measurements (`after/timings.json`, median of runs on the local Worker)

- Opening the viewer from a tile, to the scan on screen: 69 ms desktop, 99 ms tablet, 90 ms phone. The scan is already in memory from the tile; earlier 57–72 ms without the flight.
- The flight in: 95% of the way at 500 ms, when the viewer takes input and the light sweeps; settled at 1.3 s. The measured `viewerLandedMs` (1.28–1.57 s) also includes the three paused screenshot frames. The flight back: 450 ms.
- Turning the card with B: the spring overshoots once (about 20°) and is within 2° of the back by 567 ms. The evidence waits until the card is level within about a degree, which includes the slower settling of the lean after the mouse left the card: 1002 ms desktop, 884 ms tablet, 786 ms phone (`turnSettledMs`).
- Frame times while the mouse sweeps over the tilting card for two seconds: median 16.7 ms; p95 33 ms at 1440×900 and 16.8 ms at 390×844; no long tasks. Measured with a scratch harness on the development build in headless Chromium, which renders in software here; a real GPU should do no worse.
- Importing six sample pairs through the owner screen: 0.86 s, including re-encoding each scan on the device.
- Cold load to the first Items row 282 ms after, 260 ms before; section switch 72 ms after, 66 ms before. Both are within the run-to-run spread of this machine (the directory code is its own lazy chunk; the shell gained only the Administration sub-navigation).
