# V1.12 visual research and evidence: Home and practical insights

Spec: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/v1.12-operations-home.md`. Product: `docs/PRODUCT_REFERENCE.md` ("Home (V1.12)"). Release record: `docs/road-to-v2/releases/v1.12.md`. Screenshots are in `docs/visual-research/v1.12/after/`, named `home-<who>-<state>-<size>` with `desktop` 1440 px, `tablet` 768 px and `phone` 390 px (a touch screen). `after/timings.json` holds the scene's measurements; `before/` is V1.11 (`origin/main`), which has no Home, so it holds only the menus the comparison covers.

## How the evidence was made

- The real application, production build, on a throwaway local Worker and D1 (`wrangler dev --local`), every migration applied (`0001`–`0030`).
- `npm run evidence -- --out docs/visual-research/v1.12 --base origin/main --pages home`. The `home` scene (`homeScenes`, seeded by `attentionRecordsSql` and `homeRecordsSql`) adds to the 397 records the migrations seed: overdue and held loans, out-of-stock and low-stock supplies, a finding to settle (the V1.10 busy state), then repeated lending (two items, four and three times), three supplies taken out again and again, a supply with two restock requests, three reports about one place, a kit found short in two of its two checks, an item whose use was changed three times, a cataloguing session with three items for `staff.demo`, and a paused check of another place. Nothing real is used.
- It signs in as a staff member and as an Owner and captures each state at three sizes (the Owner at desktop and phone). Pictures that show a state the data cannot make (a calm hub, insights that failed) replace the answer in the browser for that picture only.
- Every image was opened and looked at.

### Defects found by looking, all fixed and re-rendered

1. **The phone's search box ended in "Search items, pla…".** The shortcut hint took the room. Phones hide the hint (they have no keyboard) and the text wraps cleanly.
2. **A lone insight card stretched to the full width on desktop**, so one row of text sat in an empty slab. The cards sit in an auto-fill grid with a minimum width, and one card keeps a card's width.
3. **The usage insight scanned the whole movement ledger.** The planner walked the item index. Pinned to the date index it reads only the 30-day window (release record, "Performance").
4. **Seven of the first picture's insights were missing.** That was the scene's seed (it looked for supplies deeper in the list than exist), not the page; the page rightly shows no card when nothing meets its rule. The seed now uses real rows, and the seven cards are in every busy picture.
5. **A screenshot showed the bottom bar across the middle of the page.** That is the full-page capture of a fixed bar, not the page; the viewport pictures (`home-*-phone`) show it where it is.

Checked by eye and kept: the biggest thing on Home is the count of what needs a person, in the serif numerals the Hub already uses for figures; urgency is a small coloured dot and the words "today" and "this week", never a red block; routine work is one quiet line ("When there is time: …") and is never added to the headline; the calm state is a green note, not an illustration; Quick actions sit beside Needs attention on desktop and below Continue on phones, so a phone shows the work first; insights sit last and read as plain sentences with their window and rule under the question; no chart, no score, no percentage (the completeness bars say "253 of 395").

### Measured by the scene (throwaway Worker, read the shape, not the absolute figure)

From `after/timings.json`: the Needs attention rows were on screen 113–171 ms after navigation and the insights 133–192 ms after it (a median of one run per size; both include Playwright's polling). A cold load to the first Items row took 301 ms and an Items → Stock switch 67 ms (the same figures as before Home, so navigation did not move). The API and query timings are in the release record.

Not done: real phones, a screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack), a Windows run, and a review by anyone but the author.

## Sources (accessed 2026-10-07)

Read in this session; the first was read through a search-result summary only, and the web had little else worth citing on this narrow subject, so the design leans on the Hub's own V1.10 model and V1.11 search.

| Source | What I took from it |
|---|---|
| [Task dashboard design guidance (ONES)](https://ones.com/blog/dashboard-design-for-task-management-layouts-that-reduce-overwhelm/), via search summary | A task home answers "what needs my attention right now?" first and shows why one item comes before another. Home leads with Needs attention, and each row says its source and how soon. |
| [Screens Design: mobile home screens](https://screensdesign.com/articles/mobile-app-home-screen-design/), via search summary | A home screen is a routing decision, not an inventory of the product: lift the most useful action and keep other destinations available without making them compete. The phone shows work first, Quick actions after, and the six sections stay in the bottom bar. |
| [Primer: empty states](https://primer.style/ui-patterns/empty-states) | Say what the empty state means in plain, factual words and avoid vague errors. The calm state says "Nothing needs a person right now" and why; the failed insights say what failed ("Insights could not be loaded") with one action. |
| [WCAG 2.2 Understanding 4.1.3 Status Messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html) | Changes are announced through a status role without moving focus. The greeting line (count of things to act on) is a polite live region and the insight failure is a status. |
| [Cloudflare D1: use indexes](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/use-indexes/) | Check plans with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` and look for SCAN. The performance test prints every insight's plan; the usage insight was the one that scanned too much. No index was added (the doctrine: no new index unless measured and needed). |
| Hub documents: `docs/PRODUCT_REFERENCE.md` ("Attention", "Global search"), the V1.10 and V1.11 visual research | One definition of "needs a person" (Attention's), one fast way in (search), and the same type, tokens and states, so Home reads as part of the app. |

## Patterns rejected

- **A chart-heavy dashboard with tiles of totals.** The spec excludes it; a total that opens nothing gives staff nothing to do. Every count on Home opens the list it counts.
- **A seventh section in the bar.** Six sections already fill a phone's bottom bar with More. Home is reached from the logo, the account menu, search ("Home") and sign-in.
- **Personal widgets, drag-and-drop layouts and remembered "favourites".** Role decides the Quick actions; nothing is stored in the browser or inferred about a person.
- **Scores, forecasts and "recommended orders".** Each insight shows the rule that selected it, its window, and the evidence; the two about supplies say "Advice only. Nothing is ordered or changed from here."
- **Loading everything at once.** The attention numbers come from the same answer the bell already fetched; the insights arrive afterwards and may fail on their own.

## What this means for Logistics Hub

Staff open the Hub to do something. Home therefore starts with what needs a person, in the same counts and the same wording as Attention (the numbers match because they are one answer), offers the work they left unfinished only when it genuinely exists, and keeps the patterns across weeks, with their window and evidence, below the work and out of the way of getting it done.
