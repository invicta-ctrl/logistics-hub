# V1.4 visual research and evidence: Smart Locations & Wayfinding

Spec: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/v1.4-smart-locations.md`. Screenshots are in `docs/visual-research/v1.4/` (`before/` is the V1.3 tip `c7d97f2`, `after/` is this branch). Everything in them is fictional: demo accounts, places invented for the shot (Office, Storage Area, Cabinet 1, Shelf 2, Garage), and pictures drawn on a canvas by the evidence script. No real photo, name or private data is in the folder.

## How the evidence was made

- The real application on a throwaway local Worker, D1 and R2 (`wrangler dev --local`) with every migration applied (`0001`–`0024`), two fictional accounts (Alex Reyes as Owner, Sam Cruz as Staff).
- `npm run evidence -- --out docs/visual-research/v1.4 --base c7d97f2 --pages items,locations`. `items` renders on both refs (before and after compare). `locations` runs only where `/api/staff/locations` exists. It builds a hierarchy through the real endpoints, gives two places drawn pictures, keeps items in places, leaves two look-alike places (as the migration would) and two items whose typed location has no place yet, files one report, then captures desktop (1440×900), tablet (768×1024, 2×) and phone (390×844, 2×): Locations tree, place sheet with and without a picture, new place, Items with the Place column, by place and "Needs a place", the item profile, the place picker with quick-add, *Where is it?* (route and picture, enlarged picture, report confirm, report sent, missing picture), the item's report card, and Self-Service on a signed-out phone (item sheet, route, report confirm, a staff-only place). Reduced motion is on for stable frames.
- Every image was opened and looked at. The first pass found the defects listed below; they were fixed and the scene captured again. Menus and the tablet copies of the denser screens were dropped to keep the folder small (5 MB).
- Measured with the script (median of seven, `timings.json`): cold load to the first Items row 249 ms after against 259 ms before; Items → Stock switch 72 ms against 76 ms. The Items list now also joins nothing on the server for places (the browser holds the whole place list, at most 500 rows), so the list answer grew only by a short `locations` array.

## Patterns adopted, and why they fit Logistics Hub

1. **A route, not a field.** Where an item is kept reads as a short vertical route whose last step is the item's place (larger, in the serif, with a filled marker), with each step's directions beneath it. The people who use it are standing in the office looking for a thing; the answer is "second floor, past the stairs → through the door → the grey cabinet → second shelf", which a breadcrumb alone cannot give. Directions are optional per step, so a place without any still shows its name.
2. **One picture for many items.** The nearest picture on the way is shown, captioned with the place it belongs to ("Cabinet 1, the place around it" for a shelf without its own). Staff photograph a cabinet once; every item in it shares it.
3. **Names carry the path.** A place picker and the Items filter label each option with its full path, because a closed menu that shows "Shelf 2" tells nobody which cabinet it is. The Items list shows the leaf place in strong text with the places around it beneath, and the path under the name on tablets and phones, where the column is dropped.
4. **Reports are two plain buttons under "Not there?".** Choosing one explains what it does and that nothing changes; staff can add a note, a phone cannot. Sent, the answer says nothing was changed. Retrying a failed send reuses the same request id.
5. **Missing things are said, not hidden.** A place without a picture says so and, for staff, links to where one is added. An item whose typed location has no place is flagged "Needs a place" with what was typed. Look-alike places are listed in a notice with the way to combine them on purpose.

## Ideas considered and rejected

- **A map or floor plan.** No floor plan exists, one office does not need one, and a drawn map would go stale. Directions in words plus a photograph answer the real question.
- **A free-text "location" next to the picker.** Two ways to say the same thing is how the near-duplicates appeared. The typed text is kept as evidence and shown once, as "Typed: …", until someone chooses a place.
- **Merging look-alike names automatically (or by case-insensitive match) in the migration.** "Cabinet 1" and "cabinet 1" could be two cabinets or one. The migration keeps them apart and says so; staff combine them with *Move items*, which writes each item's history.
- **Public pictures at thumbnail size only.** For items the large photo stays staff-only, but a person looking for a cabinet needs to recognise it. A place's picture (both sizes) is public only while staff share that place and every place above it, and only while Self-Service is open.
- **Offline reports from phones.** A report is useless once the person has left, and a queue needs its own sync rules. Reports need a connection and say so; the route itself works from the phone's last snapshot.
- **Drag-and-drop tree editing.** Slow on phones and hard to make accessible. A place is moved by choosing its parent in a form.

## Defects found by looking, and fixed

1. The place picker and filter showed the leaf name indented, so a closed menu hid which cabinet a shelf was in → options now carry the full path.
2. The picture caption said "tap to enlarge" on desktop → the cursor, the button name and the viewer say it; the caption does not.
3. The "missing picture" shot still showed a picture (a shelf inherits its cabinet's) → the scene uses a place with no picture anywhere on its route.
4. Self-Service's staff-only state showed "No picture yet — there is no place to show a picture of", which is noise when there is no route → the picture section is omitted when there is nothing to show.
5. At 200% text on a 320 px phone, place rows ran off the screen (a fixed flex basis, wide tags and per-level indentation) → the basis is capped at the row width, tags wrap, and indentation shrinks on narrow screens. Checked by `tests/browser/locations.spec.ts` at 320, 390, 768 and 1366 px.
6. The search placeholder on the Locations page was cut off on a phone ("Search places and c") → "Search places".
7. Found by the real-flow test, not the camera: a toast announced a save before the sheet had been redrawn from it, so a quick follow-up action could land on the old sheet → the toast now follows the redraw.

## Not checked

Real phones and screen readers (the structure follows the dialog pattern and the accessibility spec checks names, labels and sideways scroll, but nobody listened to it), and a place picture shown over a real office photograph (the sample pictures are drawn).
