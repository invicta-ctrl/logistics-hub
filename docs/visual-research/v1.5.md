# V1.5 visual research and evidence: Rapid Catalogue & Bulk Operations

Spec: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/v1.5-rapid-catalogue.md`. Screenshots are in `docs/visual-research/v1.5/` (`before/` is the V1.4 tip, `after/` is this branch). Everything in them is fictional: demo accounts, a shelf (Office › Cabinet 1 › Shelf 2) made for the shot, and items the demo script saved through the real endpoint ("Projector 33", "Marker 34", "Unlabelled box 36" …). The migrated catalog's own item names (acrylic paints, rope) appear because the real seed data is what the throwaway database holds. The pictures are drawn in the browser, not photographs.

## How the evidence was made

- The real application on a throwaway local Worker, D1 and R2 (`wrangler dev --local`) with every migration applied (`0001`–`0026`) and two fictional accounts.
- `npm run evidence -- --out docs/visual-research/v1.5 --base origin/road-to-v2/v1.4-smart-locations --pages items,catalogue`. The `catalogue` scene makes a place tree through the real endpoints, saves 36 items of every kind on one shelf through the real capture endpoint (a long morning already behind), and then drives the real screens at 1440, 768 and 390 px: the start page with an open session; the capture screen; a suggestion with its reason; a photo chosen and previewed; the possible-match card shown by the first press of Save; Not sure / Review later; **a save made while the browser is offline (shown unsaved) and sent when it returns**; Select mode in Items with five items ticked, and the dialog that shows what a change touches; and finishing.
- Every image was opened and looked at. The first rendered pass found the defects listed below; they were fixed and the scenes captured again. Only the screens that carry information are kept (5 MB).
- Measured with the script (median of seven, `timings.json`): cold load to the first Items row and Items → Stock switch are unchanged by this slice (the Items list gained a checkbox column only in Select mode).
- Not done: real phones, real cameras, a screen reader. The photo input uses the phone's camera through `capture="environment"`; here a file is chosen instead.

## Patterns adopted, and why they fit Logistics Hub

1. **Place first, once.** The session starts by choosing where you stand; the place stays at the top of every screen as the largest line and is the only thing a long session never re-asks. Every capture still stores its own place, so changing the shelf mid-session never rewrites earlier items. (From counting practice: fix the location, then the item.)
2. **One form that clears itself.** Photo, name, how it is used, how many, category, unit, save. There is no table to return to: Save clears the form and puts the cursor back in the name. The save goes to a short queue shown below the form, so the next item starts at once even on slow Wi-Fi.
3. **Item behaviour is four big buttons, never a default.** Borrow & return, Consume, Use gradually, Not sure / Review later: two lines each (what it is, and in plain words what it means), 64 px tall, 1–4 on a keyboard. Nothing is pressed until the person presses it, because one shelf holds all three kinds.
4. **Suggestions are a dashed outline and a reason, not a decision.** "Suggested: Consume · Office Supplies · piece. Like “Whiteboard Marker Black” and 1 more. Use these". The suggested button gets a dashed border and a "Suggested" tag; one tap on *Use these* fills the fields it names (including the stock area, which sits behind *More details*, so it is named first). With no look-alike, the session's own repetition is offered ("Same as your last two items"). Nothing is saved or selected by a suggestion.
5. **Possible matches are shown, not enforced.** A warm card under the name lists up to three matches with *why* ("Same name", "Same serial number", "Its photo looks the same", "Almost the same name"), their count and place, and a link that opens the item in a new tab so the capture is not lost. The first press of Save only shows it; the button then says **Save as a separate item**. Two of the same thing are normal on a shelf.
6. **Review later is a first-class answer.** "Not sure" keeps the photo, the name, the place and the count and makes category and unit optional ("optional for now"). Such a record is stored unclassified and appears in the Review later list on the Catalogue page; it cannot reach the Lending Hub or Self-Service.
7. **A failed save is a state on screen.** Each row says *Saving…*, *Saved*, *Not saved yet* (amber, "It will be sent when the connection is back"), *Saved, photo to send*, or *Needs you* (with Edit, Save as a separate item, Discard). The top bar says "All saved" or "2 waiting to send", and on a phone a *See* link jumps to the list. Unsent items are on the device, so a reload keeps them.
8. **Select mode, one change, shown first.** Items gains a *Select* button; checkboxes appear only then. A sticky bar says how many are selected and offers Move to…, Category…, Stock area…, Mark reviewed, Clear. The dialog shows what will change (the count, the first names, how many already have it, how many will be skipped and why) *before* the button, which names the count ("Move 600 items"). Quantities are stated not to change.
9. **Making them official is its own step.** Items captured with a classification are saved as *needing review*, so they are not offered to phones the moment they are saved. Finishing offers one button, "Mark 30 items reviewed", for exactly the classified ones.

## Ideas considered and rejected

- **Barcode or QR scanning.** Most of the office's stock has no barcode, and the spec's flow is photo, name, behaviour, quantity. A scanner can be added later as another way to fill the name; it is not needed to catalogue a shelf.
- **A session-wide "everything here is a Consumable" default.** The spec forbids it and it is wrong for a mixed shelf: a locked default is how a projector ends up consumable.
- **Auto-saving a suggested value, or pre-selecting it.** Even a very likely suggestion can be wrong for this item; a pre-selected button is pressed by habit. It is outlined and explained, and the person presses.
- **Blocking a duplicate.** Twelve identical chairs and a second printer are legitimate. The card warns; the second press proceeds.
- **Merging into the existing item.** Quantities change through movements and counts, not through a cataloguing shortcut; the card links to the item instead.
- **A "Saved!" blocking dialog after each item.** It would cost a tap per item. The cleared form, the count and the row below are the confirmation.
- **Swipe gestures and long-press selection.** Hard to discover, impossible with a keyboard, and easy to trigger by accident. A visible *Select* button and checkboxes are slower to describe and safer.
- **Offline cataloguing.** V1.6. This slice keeps unsent items on the device so nothing is lost, but it does not try to work without the server, and the screen says so ("Not saved yet").

## Defects found by looking (or by the first runs of the tests), and fixed

Found while building and by the automated runs:
1. The hidden photo input took the width every input takes and pushed the whole page sideways at every size (found by the layout test); it is now one pixel wide.
2. Finishing a session navigated to the address already shown, which the router ignores, so the summary never appeared (found by the browser test); the page is now drawn again.
3. Field ids in the script used two spellings (`cap-` and `cat-`), so the screen stopped at its first line (found by the first browser run).
4. An item saved a moment ago was not noticed as a possible match until the next 30-second refresh of the list (found by the real-Worker end-to-end run); the page now remembers what it has captured.

Found by opening the rendered screenshots:
5. **Possible-match card flooded.** Typing "Acrylic Paint" listed four of its colours as "Almost the same name" and pushed the choices off the screen. A name now matches another only if it is one word longer or shorter (or very close overall), and at most three matches are shown.
6. **Category chips wrapped** into two or three lines of stacked pills ("Cleaning Supplies & Equipment", "Consumable Items (For USC Only)"). Four chips, one line each, with an ellipsis.
7. **Phone: the shelf was cut off** ("Office › Cabine…"), the one fact a person must trust. The place takes its own row on a phone and wraps instead of cutting.
8. **Phone: the four choices stacked** into 4 rows (about 600 px) and the two save buttons took a quarter of the screen. The choices are two by two, and the save buttons share one row.
9. **Phone: a waiting item was far below the form.** The top line said "2 waiting to send" but the row explaining it was a long scroll away. A *See* link now appears while anything is waiting or needs a decision.
10. **Bulk dialog sentence** ran the new place into the item names ("…Shelf 2: 1/8 Illustration Board…"). The names moved to their own line.
11. **Evidence script** re-used the previous scene's photo, which the app rightly flagged as a possible match; each scene now starts from a clean form.

## Known limits

- Real phones, real cameras and screen readers were not tested.
- The "Photo looks the same" hint compares a 64-bit difference hash made in the browser. It is a hint for re-shot objects, not a guarantee; two identical-looking objects photographed alike will warn, and two photos of one object from different angles will not.
- On a phone, a long list of captures is far below the form by design (the form is what is used). The *See* link and the count at the top are the way to it.
