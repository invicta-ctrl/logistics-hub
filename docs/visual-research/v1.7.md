## Patterns adopted, and why they fit Logistics Hub

1. **One place at a time, against what the records expect there** (clinicedc, ServiceNow). A check covers a place and the places inside it: the shelf you stand at, or the whole store room. Two people can't check one place at once; the Catalog names who is.
2. **Three answers per item, one tap for the common one.** **Here · 4** states the number it confirms, so a tap is an assertion of the count, not a vague "seen". **Count differs** opens a stepper; **Can’t find** is the *missing* state. The rarer **Record looks wrong** sits behind ⋯ beside the name (the spec's review-needed state; amendment §11: it never changes classification).
3. **Unexpected is first-class** (clinicedc's *unexpected*): **Found something not on the list** searches the saved catalog (works offline) and records **Found here**, or **Not in the catalog** with a name. Both stay findings; nothing is created or moved by seeing them.
4. **Progress in numbers and a bar, findings counted beside it** (Pio): "7 / 12 checked · 3 findings", sticky while scrolling, with To check / Checked / Findings tabs. The list starts on To check and moves to Checked when everything is done.
5. **Counting and applying are separate** (Odoo's Apply, cycle-count approval): finishing a check changes nothing. The review, signed in, settles each finding: **Post count** (a normal count movement with the check named in its note), **Move it here**, **Report its location**, or **Leave it as it is** with a reason. Each decision is recorded with who and when; the observation itself is never changed.
6. **Recount instead of trusting a stale figure** (Mintsoft's 2nd counts, HotWax's recount queue): when stock moved between what the counter saw and the review (a loan, a delivery, another device offline), the review says **Stock changed since this was counted** and asks for what is there now, posted against the figure on screen (the ledger's own guard refuses anything else).
7. **A handover summary** (Thrive, Odoo): who, when, how long; expected / checked / here / findings / not checked; each finding with what was decided; **Print summary** strips the controls.
8. **Offline is normal** (V1.6, Open Health Stack): the check opens from the device, marks wait as **Not saved yet** / **N waiting to send** (V1.6's terms), and the bar light says **Offline**.

## Ideas considered and rejected

- **Blind counts** (no expected figure shown). They reduce confirmation bias, but for a student-council office checking a few dozen items a shelf, showing the list and the number lets most items be confirmed in one tap; the tap states the number, and a recount is asked for whenever stock moved. Revisit if counts prove unreliable.
- **Auto-applying small variances** (variance thresholds). Every stock change here stays a deliberate act with a name on it (spec: observations never silently rewrite quantity).
- **Barcode or QR scanning.** Items carry no tags today; search-as-you-type over the saved catalog serves the "found something" case.
- **Posting a count from Found here.** A few found on another shelf say nothing about the item's whole stock: the review offers Move it here or Report, and a count belongs to a check of the item's own place.
- **A separate audit app.** Checking lives in the Catalog: the same install, offline access, frame and sender.

## Defects found by looking, and fixed

1. The progress bar was full at 0 / 12: its width was an inline style, which the app's Content-Security-Policy blocks. It is a native `<progress>` now (accessible by itself).
2. The sticky progress header was translucent, so rows ghosted behind "7 / 12 checked": now solid with a rule under it.
3. Each row spent a whole line on "Record looks wrong": now ⋯ beside the name, which made a shelf noticeably faster to scan.
4. Every finding showed a reason field and No change: now folded under **Leave it as it is**.
5. On a computer **Post this count** stretched the full width: it sizes to its label.
6. The review listed "Not in the catalog: Label printer" twice. Not cosmetic: the found-something sheet added a click handler on every opening, so its second use saved twice. One handler now; a browser test checks each answer is sent once.
7. After **Move it here**, the finding said it had been "recorded at" its new place: the review reads the place recorded on the observation (Worker test).
8. With **Count differs** open, Save count sat under the bottom bar: an opened row is brought fully into view.
9. At 320 px with 200% text the check overflowed (the three answers and tabs were fixed columns, and long labels didn't wrap): they wrap now (accessibility test).

Found by the end-to-end and browser tests before any screenshot: a page opened offline still thought it was offline after reconnecting, so finishing there showed "not saved on this device" (the connection is read live now); and a refresh from the server dropped what the device hadn't sent yet, so progress went backwards (unsent observations are laid over the server's answer).
