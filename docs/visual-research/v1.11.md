# V1.11 visual research and evidence: global search and item links

Spec: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/v1.11-intelligent-search.md`. Product: `docs/PRODUCT_REFERENCE.md` ("Global search (V1.11)"). Release record: `docs/road-to-v2/releases/v1.11.md`. Screenshots are in `docs/visual-research/v1.11/`, named `search-<who>-<state>-<size>` with `desktop` 1440 px, `tablet` 768 px and `phone` 390 px (a touch screen). `timings.json` holds the scene's measurements.

## How the evidence was made

- The real application, production build, on a throwaway local Worker and D1 (`wrangler dev --local`), every migration applied (`0001`–`0030`).
- `npm run evidence -- --out docs/visual-research/v1.11 --pages search`. The `search` scene (`searchScenes`, seeded by `searchRecordsSql`) adds to the 397 records the migrations seed: 110 numbered archive boxes (so the catalog has **508 items**), ten places up to three levels deep (one with a 68-character name, one turned off), four kits (one with a long name), eight item links of every kind, and 64 fictional Staff Directory people with `00-SAMPLE-nnn` numbers. Nothing real is used.
- It signs in once as an Owner and once as a staff member, then captures each state at three sizes. Phones are emulated as touch screens, so they show what a phone shows (no keyboard hints). The loading and session-ended pictures hold back or replace the index answer in the browser for that picture only.

### Defects found by looking (or by probing the rendered page), all fixed and re-rendered

1. **The field had the app's focus ring inside the panel**, a second frame around the text. Inside the palette focus never leaves the field, so the caret and the active row show where it is.
2. **"cabinet" listed items kept in a cabinet above the cabinets themselves.** A place or kit named what was typed is the narrower answer: it now leads, and its items follow, each saying where it is.
3. **After opening a result, focus fell to the page body**, so a keyboard user started again from the top. The item, kit and place sheets now keep focus on Close when they redraw, and the palette no longer pulls focus back to its button after it navigates. (Plain pages, such as the directory, land where a link in the navigation lands: on the body, as before V1.11.)
4. **An administrator heard no result count**: the directory's answer cancelled the pending announcement. The count is now announced once the whole answer is in, including the people.
5. **The Linked items card laid its rows out sideways**: it reused class names that the Staff Directory's account link card styles. It has its own names.
6. **Picking an item in the link form also fired the photo tile's handler** (a shared `data-pick` attribute). The link form uses `data-link-pick`.
7. **At 200% text on a 320 px phone the new search button pushed the bar off the screen** (caught by the real-Worker shell test). At that size the brand mark gives way (Items is in the bottom bar) and search keeps a 44 px target.
8. **"santos" marked only "Santo"**: query words are folded to the singular, and the mark stopped short. A plural's "s" is now marked with its word (browser test added).
9. **The link picker offered "Stapler - Small" above "Staples - Small" for "staples small".** Whole-word matches now come before word starts.
10. **The phone pictures showed ↑ ↓ ↵ Esc hints.** That was the scene, which did not emulate a touch screen; real phones (`hover: none`) hide the row. The scene now renders phones as touch devices.
11. **An administrator searching a common surname got eight people and no way to the rest.** The group ends with **See all N in the Staff Directory**, which opens the directory's own search with the query.

Checked by eye and kept: one result per row with its kind on the right (desktop) and a one-line reason under the name ("Goes in Sewing Kit · Storage Room", "Kept in Logistics Office › Cabinet 1 › Shelf B", "In the kit …"); long names wrap to two lines and then end with "…"; the reason line ends with "…" on desktop and wraps on phones; groups hold 4–6 rows before **Show all**, or 12 when one kind is alone; nothing shows a score or a percentage; the scrim is the one the sheets use.

### Measured by the scene (throwaway Worker, read the shape, not the absolute figure)

From `timings.json`: 508 items, 10 places, 4 kits, 8 links. The index is 104,117 bytes of JSON and **7,005 bytes gzipped** (this catalog's names repeat a lot; the synthetic tiers in the release record compress less). The first search after sign-in, from Ctrl+K to the first result on screen, including fetching the palette's code and the index: **211 ms** (median of five). A later search (a 304 for the index): **148 ms**. Both include Playwright's own polling. Navigation did not move: a cold load to the first Items row took 438 ms and an Items → Stock switch 84 ms (V1.10 recorded 461 / 433 ms and 91 / 89 ms), because the palette is fetched only on first use. The ranking, index and directory costs at 500, 5,000 and 10,000 items are in the release record.

Not done: real phones, a screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack), a Windows run, and a review by anyone but the author.

## Patterns rejected

- **A chat box or natural-language commands.** The spec and the amendment rule them out, and a predictable list is faster. A few fixed words ("overdue", "low stock", "unclassified") open existing pages; nothing is parsed.
- **Scores, percentages or "best match" badges.** Results say why they matched in words.
- **Learned ranking, "popular" lists and recent searches.** They move results under people's habits, store behaviour, and could reveal who looked up whom.
- **A separate command palette beside search,** and **prefix syntax** as the only way to scope. One field finds records and pages.
- **Infinite results.** At most 50 per kind, with the true count.
- **Shipping the Staff Directory to the browser.** People are ranked on the Worker for administrators only, eight at a time, and never kept after the dialog closes.

## What this means for Logistics Hub

Staff look for a thing, a place or a kit while someone waits at the counter. Search therefore answers in the browser without a request per key, says in each row what the result is and why it is there (the place it is kept, the kit it is in, the item it is used with), keeps the same order for the same words, and opens exactly what was chosen. The links staff record on an item (used with, alternative to, replaced by, holds) are what make a search for one thing surface the other.
