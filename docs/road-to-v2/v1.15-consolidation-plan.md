## Owner amendment: mobile-first and perceived-performance pass (Earl, 2026-10-07)

Earl strengthened V1.15 with a finalization pass. It adds acceptance criteria and checks; it adds no feature and no framework, and all accepted V1.15 scope stays. **No V1.15 implementation starts until V1.14's gate is met.** That includes the dead-export removal in the findings below, which is held. It is recorded as an accepted amendment, on Earl's "yes" of 2026-10-07: `docs/specs/accepted/road-to-v2/2026-10-07-v1.15-mobile-perceived-performance-amendment.md`, referenced from the V1.15 spec.

Intent: V2 must feel immediate, not only benchmark fast. Mobile is the primary acceptance target and desktop second.

**Ownership.** V1.14 stays authoritative for the performance architecture: D1 and query efficiency, bounded APIs, DOM, storage and queues, Cloudflare use and scale tests. After V1.14 completes, V1.15 inherits its measured budgets and must not regress them. A remaining bottleneck that a V1.15 journey exposes is repaired as a V2 quality defect with the smallest measured fix, and no speculative performance infrastructure is added.

**Mobile-first widths.** Check about 320, 360, 390 and 430 px first, then tablet, then desktop. Look for clipping, overlap, sideways overflow, offscreen or unreachable actions, bad wrapping, small touch targets, broken tab layouts, awkward dialogs and sheets, unsafe-area problems and uneven spacing. Desktop must use the room cleanly without looking sparse or stretched.

**Perceived performance.**
- Draw the stable page shell and geometry at once; never leave an unexplained blank region.
- Content-shaped skeletons only on a true first load. After valid content is shown, keep it during a refresh instead of swapping in a loader.
- Load primary data before secondary metrics and media. Optional panels fail on their own and never block the page.
- Images reserve their size and load lazily and asynchronously.
- Reuse what exists (intent-based route warming, request de-duplication, ETags, failure isolation) rather than building a second system.
- Every interaction gets an immediate visual acknowledgement. Durable writes (stock, loans, audits, accounts, system settings) may show a working state at once, but show success only after the server has committed.
- Privacy audit before any new persistence or caching. Official staff IDs, private evidence and other restricted data are never cached persistently for speed.

**Interaction completeness.** For every route, list each visible control. Each must be reachable, named, usable and uncovered at phone and desktop sizes, and either exercised by a fixture-safe browser test or covered by a V2 journey. No dead button, dead tab, placeholder action or unexplained disabled control remains.

Design decisions (smallest durable):

1. **No migration.** Resolve and Ignore are written as `audit_log` rows on the Self-Service record (a new reviewed-identity action in the Activity vocabulary); the Attention reason lists records with a missing or invalid identity that have no such row. `idx_audit_log_entity_item` already serves that lookup. If the lookup is not bounded at scale, V1.15 asks V1.14 for a migration number (V1.14 may claim 0031 for a closed-loans index) and does not take one on its own.
2. **Strict Student ID applies to new Self-Service records only.** `STUDENT_ID_PATTERN` stays as it is for the Staff Directory and staff-entered loans, so no historical record is rewritten or made unreadable. A queued offline record from an older phone that reaches the server without a valid identity is accepted once, idempotently, and shown in Identity needs review; it is not refused, because that would lose evidence.
3. **Linking needs no new data.** Takes and Uses carry the Student ID from now on, so the existing exact-ID rule links them. A first name with an exact ID links and also appears in Identity needs review. Two directory people with the same ID cannot exist (the column is unique), so matching cannot be ambiguous.
4. **Historical formats are not yet checked in production.** The production read was refused, so the shapes of existing IDs in `staff_directory`, `loans` and `self_service_events` are unknown to me. This does not change the design above (it never touches old rows) but decides how many existing staff records will link to a new 8-digit entry. Earl is asked to allow a shape-only count or run it himself.

### Deferred V1.11 measurement (amendment §14), run 2026-10-07 19:04–19:08 UTC

Command: `npm run evaluate:suggestions -- --ai` on the public seed catalog (254 confirmed items, leave-one-out). Payload: `aiPayload` only (typed name, other names, live option lists). No private export and no per-item output was kept.

**Harness defect found first.** Workers AI now answers these models in the chat-completion shape (`choices[0].message.content`). V1.11's `readAiAnswer` only read `response`, so the first run counted every one of 213 replies as "no answer". `aiReplyText` reads both shapes (`f9fc37d`); the numbers below are the run with the corrected reader.

| | behaviour | unit | category |
|---|---|---|---|
| Items needing help (built-in not Strong) | 173 | 189 | 151 |
| Built-in right | 71 | 96 | 75 |
| AI answered | 173 (100%) | 189 (100%) | 151 (100%) |
| AI right (non-null precision) | 82 (47%) | 45 (24%) | 19 (13%) |
| Built-in Weak tier: built-in right / AI right | 67% / 38% | 80% / 21% | 84% / 5% |
| Recovered with no built-in suggestion | 34 of 67 | 8 of 62 | 14 of 61 |
| Built-in or AI right | 118 | 115 | 95 |

Calls 213; HTTP errors 0; timeouts 0; replies that were not JSON 0; values outside the options 0. Latency p50 1.2 s, p95 1.6 s, max 6.3 s. Neurons: 137.9 for the run (0.65 a call), reported by Workers AI; the account total for the day after both runs and a 30-call sample was 279 Neurons (GraphQL).

**Result: negative.** Granite never abstains, and where the built-in suggestion exists it is right far less often than the built-in one. USC's categories ("For sale items", "Consumable items (for USC only)", "Pantry") encode policy, not what a thing is, so no model can read them from a name. Decision under §14 ("a negative result is acceptable"): **no model-chosen category or unit ships.** The V1.11 classification second opinion stays off. Behaviour recovery on items with no built-in suggestion (34 of 67, three options) is not good enough to show either. Granite's remaining amendment roles (aliases, normalization of short text to an existing catalog item) are kept only where they map to values the Hub already has, and each is measured before it is shown.

### Not built, and why

- **Qwen workflow and GLM:** no workflow in the product needs a model to plan or reason. The amendment says not to force them, so there is no code for either.
- **Granite aliases and normalization:** the measurement above showed Granite cannot be trusted with this catalog's own words; no consumer is built until a measured one exists.
- **AI Gateway:** Workers observability, the aggregate log line and the GraphQL Neurons dataset already give the evidence. Gateway request logging is on by default, which would store images. It stays an optional owner step, logging off.
- **Staff-facing AI surface:** no tab, prompt box, model choice or percentage. A person sees a filled name with a hint, a look-alike in the list they already read, or an Attention entry.

## Findings

### V1.15's own files

1. **Dead exports, no references anywhere: removed in `929a26a`.** `mayGive` (`src/account-ui.ts`), `REORDER_STATUSES` (`src/catalog-policy.ts`), `SOURCE_KEY` (`src/directory-policy.ts`), `SHORTCUT_TEXT` (`src/search-palette.ts`), `ROLE_LABELS` (`src/staff.ts`). About 90 more exports are used only inside their own file; those stay as they are, because dropping `export` changes nothing at runtime.
2. **Terminology (amendment §4):** no "Consume", "Consumed or used", internal enum name or percentage appears in UI copy. The only `%` shown is the ID viewer's zoom level. This is clean.
3. **"Catalog" and "Catalogue" both appear on screen.** "Catalog" is the installable app and the Administration section; "Catalogue" is the page inside it (its title is "Catalogue · Catalog"); "cataloguing" is the act. Two spellings of one word read as a typo. **This needs Earl's decision** (wording on screens people know). The recommended default is to keep "Catalog" for the app and section, rename the page to **Add items** (Home and search already call it that), and keep "cataloguing" as the verb.

4. **Phone audit, first pass (2026-10-07, real Worker, owner account, 19 routes at 320, 360, 390, 430, 768 and 1440 px):** no route scrolls sideways at any width. Fixed: at 320 px the staff bar title clipped to "Logistics H…" (now the mark alone stands for it below 336 px), the Catalog bar's "HAU USC LOGI…" (hidden below 360 px, as Self-Service already does), and view and tab strips cut their last label mid-word ("Returne", "QR c"); a strip that does not fit now fades on the side that has more, driven by its own scroll, so a strip that fits is unchanged. Administration > Self-Service still said Take and Use ask "A name. No photo." and named the database setting; it now states the identity rule and drops the internal term. Remaining small targets are text links in sentences (exempt under WCAG 2.5.8) and the 16 px "Show inactive" checkboxes, whose label makes the target larger.

5. **Staff Directory linking (amendment G):** `owned()` in `src/staff-directory.ts` now links a loan or phone record to a person only by the exact student ID on their profile (outer spaces and letter case aside); the typed name is never a key, so "Ana" with Ana Santos's ID is hers, a different person's name cannot move it, and a person with no ID on file has no linked records. Usage also lists phone uses (they move no stock, so the ledger never had them). The "Matched by" column went with the name rule. `tests/staff-directory.test.ts` covers each case in amendment K.

6. **Other long history panels (amendment J):** an item's History tab shows its recent entries and links to Activity; Loans > Returned shows the newest 100; Self-Service shows at most 200 records; Directory usage could draw up to 1,000 table rows on the profile, so it now draws 100 at a time with Show more (totals still count every row). No generic scroller was added.

7. **First loads on a slow connection (2026-10-07, real Worker, 390 px, every API answer held 2 s, 16 routes):** opening or refreshing any staff page, the Catalog included, showed a blank screen until the session answered. It now shows the bar and the page's shape at once (`src/main.ts`, first load only; in-app moves keep the page they leave). Layout shift once data arrived was 0.25 on Items, 0.19 on Kits and 0.18 on Administration > Catalog: "Connecting…" turning into "Updated 3:57 AM" re-wrapped the header buttons, the review meter and the view strip appeared from nothing, and the coverage placeholder was a third of the real height. With the space held for each, every route measures 0.10 or less (Items 0.05, Kits 0.10, Catalog 0.07, the rest under 0.04).

8. **Take and Use proof photos against the V1.14 resource model (amendment E, stop condition).** A model, not a measurement, on V1.14's own bands (green at 20% or less, caution above 35%, protective at 50%). Each Take or Use now uploads one photo, shrunk on the phone to at most 1600 px at JPEG quality 0.82 (`shrinkPhoto`, cap 2 MB). Sample photos encoded the same way came to about 0.155 bytes per pixel, so a full-size phone photo is about 300 KB; camera noise makes 400 KB a fair planning figure. Requests and operations stay small: one upload and one Class A write per record, far under 1% of the Workers and R2 allowances. Storage is the term that moves: phone photos are kept until the owner removes old personal details (one year after a record is settled), so at 20 Takes and Uses a day the added photos reach about 2.9 GB in a year (29% of R2's 10 GB, between green and caution), at 40 a day about 5.8 GB (58%, past protective), on top of borrow and return photos. Self-Service's real daily volume is not known here. **Earl's decision:** keep 1600 px, or shrink Self-Service proof photos (for example 1280 px at quality 0.75, about half the bytes), or remove Take and Use photos sooner than a year. Nothing was changed while this is open. The real figure to read is the EVIDENCE bucket's size in Cloudflare's R2 dashboard.
