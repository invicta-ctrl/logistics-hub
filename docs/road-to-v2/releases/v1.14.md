## Scale fixtures and the query-plan audit

`tests/scale-fixture.ts` builds deterministic hubs: current size (500 items, 6,000 movements, 500 loans, 3,000 audit rows, 1,000 Self-Service events), 10x (5,000 items) and, with `SCALE_STRESS=1`, 100x (50,000 items, 600,000 movements). `tests/scale-hot-paths.test.ts` runs 14 staff reads at each tier, records every statement each read issues with its bound values, runs `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on it, and fails if any read walks `inventory_movements`, `audit_log`, `loans` or `self_service_events` (except the open-reviews partial index, which holds only open work). It also fails if `recentActivity`, `attentionSummary`, `insights` or `findPeople` grow more than 1.5x in size at 10x. Before the fixes, 6 of 43 cases failed (the two scans above); now all pass. `npm test` runs the 1x and 10x tiers.

Times are node:sqlite in memory, one run each, on a loaded cloud container: they show shape, not D1 latency. D1 does not report `rows_read` here, so row cost is argued from the plans, not counted.

| Read | 500 items | 5,000 items | 50,000 items |
|---|---|---|---|
| `staffInventory` (whole catalog) | 16 ms, 542 KB | 115 ms, 3.2 MB | 1277 ms, 29.8 MB |
| `stockOverview` | 20 ms, 578 KB | 129 ms, 3.3 MB | 1354 ms, 31.2 MB |
| `catalogueSnapshot` | 8 ms, 248 KB | 51 ms, 1.5 MB | 579 ms, 13.7 MB |
| `searchIndex` | 3 ms, 172 KB | 22 ms, 1.0 MB | 149 ms, 9.6 MB |
| `attention` | 14 ms, 61 KB | 93 ms, 120 KB | 973 ms, 120 KB |
| `attentionSummary` | 5 ms, 2 KB | 33 ms, 2 KB | 346 ms, 2 KB |
| `insights` | 8 ms, 2 KB | 71 ms, 2 KB | 975 ms, 2 KB |
| `loansOverview` | 8 ms, 73 KB | 42 ms, 109 KB | 498 ms, 472 KB |
| `selfServiceReview` | 24 ms, 5 KB | 177 ms, 48 KB | 1643 ms, 96 KB |
| `recentActivity` | 2 ms, 22 KB | 5 ms, 22 KB | 30 ms, 23 KB |
| `directory` | 1 ms, 25 KB | 4 ms, 256 KB | 33 ms, 2.5 MB |
| `findPeople` | 1 ms, 0.2 KB | 7 ms, 0.2 KB | 53 ms, 0.2 KB |
| `kitList` | 2 ms, 5 KB | 2 ms, 52 KB | 3 ms, 53 KB |
| `locationList` | 1 ms, 13 KB | 5 ms, 134 KB | 63 ms, 1.3 MB |

Honest reading:
- The scan fixes changed the plans, not the wall time in memory (`selfServiceReview` at 100x: 1805 ms before, 1643 ms after). Their value is on D1, where `rows_read` counts rows scanned.
- **What the scan check proves.** `tests/scale-hot-paths.test.ts` plans every statement of the 14 reads at each tier, maps plan aliases to tables and fails on a scan of a history table (movements, audit log, loans, Self-Service events, balances) unless that read and table are on its short exception list: Attention, its summary and Self-Service review read the whole ledger through the balance view; the activity feed and stock overview read movements; the loans overview scans the closed-loan list. The last needs a `loans(closed_at)` index, a new migration (0031, an owner action at merge time); it is not added because rows scanned are not measurable locally.
- **Bounded answers, linear work.** `attention`, `insights`, `attentionSummary` and `selfServiceReview` return small answers, but their work grows with the catalog and with the activity inside their windows (30/90 days). That is by design (windows, not history) and is fine at the current size; at 100x the cost is high (about 1 s here).
- **Whole-catalog answers are an explicit exception.** `staffInventory`, `stockOverview`, `catalogueSnapshot`, `searchIndex`, `directory` and `locationList` return the whole collection because staff search, filters, the offline catalogue and the command palette run in the browser on the full list. Justification: they are revision-checked (`ETag`, a 304 costs one row), the full list is fetched once per change, and the fixture's gzip ratio (35:1 at 500 items, 15.7 KB for 555 KB) is optimistic because its rows repeat; real data compresses less. At 10x the uncompressed answer is 3.2 MB. Revisit if the catalog passes about 3,000 items: the next step would be a server-filtered list, which is a new feature and needs an amendment.
- **The inventory balance is computed from the whole ledger on every full inventory load** (the `inventory_balances` view sums every posted movement per item). This is the movement-derived rule working as designed and is not changed. It does make a full load cost rows proportional to the ledger, so the number of full loads matters; see the resource model.

## Cloudflare resource envelope

Limits re-read from Cloudflare's pages on 2026-10-07 (Workers page last updated 2026-09-05): Workers Free 100,000 requests/day, 10 ms CPU, 50 subrequests; D1 Free 5 million rows read/day, 100,000 written/day, 5 GB; R2 Free 10 GB-month, 1 million Class A, 10 million Class B a month. Unchanged from the doctrine's snapshot. Bands: green at or below 20%, caution above 35%, protective at 50%.

A model, not a measurement. Assumptions (stated so they can be corrected): 8 staff, each with a page open and visible 4 h a day; 3 Self-Service phones left open 10 h; 100 phone visits a day; 100 writes a day (stock, loans, Self-Service syncs; each bumps the catalog revision).

| Term | Before V1.14 | After (page idle) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff page checks | 8 x 4 h x 420 = 13,440/day | 8 x 4 h x 180 = 5,760 | 10 s page check plus 60 s attention check per page (180 s once quiet) |
| Self-Service phones left open | 3 x 10 h x 120 = 3,600 | 3 x 10 h x 40 = 1,200 | 30 s check, 90 s while a record awaits staff |
| Phone visits (page, catalog, thumbnails) | about 4,000 | about 4,000 | thumbnails cached by the phone for an hour |
| Staff actions | about 2,000 | about 2,000 | |
| **Worker requests** | **about 23,000 (23%)** | **about 13,000 (13%)** | green after; above green before |

With 20 staff pages open instead of 8, before is about 43,000 (43%, above caution) and after about 22,000 (22%). Staff page count is the term that moves the result most; the backoff is the remedy that was measured to work.

D1 rows read: a plain check is about 4 rows (session, account, setting, revision). Before this review fix that was not true of Attention: each open page recomputed it (the whole ledger and a Self-Service scan) every 60 s. It now returns a 304 for one row unless the revision or the hour changed, and slows with the other checks. The other heavy term is full loads after a change: every open Items page refetches the whole inventory when the revision moves, and each load reads the ledger. At the 500-item fixture that is about 6,000 ledger rows plus the items (the real Hub's ledger is smaller today: the migrated ledger is one row per item plus what staff recorded since). With 8 pages open and 100 changes a day that is up to 800 full loads a day: about 5 million rows a day at 6,000 ledger rows per load, which would exceed the free allowance, and about 0.3 million at 400 ledger rows. **The real figure is the first thing to read from Cloudflare's D1 analytics** (rows read per day) before the Hub's ledger passes a few thousand rows. If it is above 35% of 5 million, the doctrine's order applies: bound the caller's scope first (a page needs only the rows it shows), then a reconstructable cache keyed by revision, and only then a read model. None was built here because the measured problem is not yet shown on real data (complexity gate, question 1), and the Cache API does not work on `workers.dev` hosts, so a cache cannot be assumed.

R2: Class B reads come from photo views (thumbnails, an hour of browser cache; staff photos a day). 4,000 thumbnail reads a day is 120,000 a month, 1.2% of the Class B allowance. Class A (writes) is photos and evidence on save: far below 1%. No bucket listing is done on any request path. Storage: photos are 480 px and 1600 px JPEGs under 150 KB and 1 MB.

Workers AI: the second opinion has no binding, route or page (`tests/catalog-ai.test.ts` asserts it), so AI usage is zero and a disabled setting, timeout or exhausted allowance cannot affect cataloguing. Nothing was added.

## Cache, media and security review (read, not penetration-tested)

- Service-worker caches hold only the shell and the brand/QR files. `/api/` is never intercepted, so no private data or photo enters a service-worker cache, and the media cache cannot grow with the catalog.
- Staff photos: `private, max-age=86400`, ids are random and a replacement gets a new id. Public thumbnails: answer only while the item is listed or Self-Service offers it and is open, `public, max-age=3600`, `ETag`/304; a withdrawn photo can linger on a phone for up to an hour (documented in the code).
- Staff-ID card scans live in their own bucket and are streamed through staff-only routes; they are not in any cache. The Staff Directory's people search only reaches an administrator.
- The restore check and its dump are local files under `data/private/`; nothing is uploaded.
- Not done: a test that proves no private URL is publicly cacheable by a CDN, and a review of remembered Self-Service identity beyond V1.9's own record.

## Known limitations

- Browser main-thread time, memory trend over repeated navigation, layout shift and animation jank were not measured; only DOM size was (2,422 nodes at 100 rows of 6,000). Scope items 6 and 12 are partly met.
- Item 13 (Android and iPhone/iPad install and update) was not revalidated on real devices.
- D1 `rows_read` and Worker CPU time are not available locally; the resource model is an estimate from stated assumptions and must be checked against Cloudflare's analytics (see above).
- The whole-catalog answers and the full-ledger balance are justified exceptions, not fixed. The loans overview closed-list scan needs a `loans(closed_at)` index (migration 0031, owner action); not created.
- The "quiet" backoff applies to staff pages only after six unchanged checks; the Attention page keeps its 15 s pace until then.
- The 12-month limit on known borrowers is a small behaviour change.
- The `docs/visual-research/v1.14/` images are four: no real phone, no screen reader, no dark theme.
- The V1.13 record on `main` carries a stale integration line (see PREDECESSOR GATE).
